CRA reporting is now live: the ENISA Single Reporting Platform explained

The first CRA obligations are no longer on the horizon. Since 11 September 2026, manufacturers have to report — here is what that means in practice.

For most of the Cyber Resilience Act, December 2027 is the date everyone talks about. But one part of the regulation has already arrived: the duty to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents (Article 14) has applied since 11 September 2026. On the same day, ENISA launched the CRA Single Reporting Platform (SRP) — the one place where those reports go.

The key point: reporting applies to products with digital elements that are on the EU market now — not just to products designed after 2027. If you sell connected products or software in the EU, you are already in scope for this part of the CRA.

What is the Single Reporting Platform?

The SRP is ENISA's portal for CRA notifications. Instead of working out which national authority to contact, a manufacturer submits one report. It is received by the CSIRT of the Member State where the manufacturer has its main establishment, made available to ENISA, and shared onward with other relevant CSIRTs so they can warn and protect users.

  • Portal: portal.cra-srp.enisa.europa.eu
  • Status: operational since 11 September 2026 (initial operating capability), and upgraded to version 1.4 on 17 November 2026.
  • Help: ENISA publishes an FAQ, a glossary, a user manual, a tutorial video and a factsheet alongside the platform.

What must be reported — and when

Two kinds of events trigger a report: an actively exploited vulnerability in your product (there is evidence a malicious actor is exploiting it), and a severe incident that affects the security of your product. Each follows a staged timeline:

24 hours
Early warning — as soon as possible and within 24 hours of becoming aware.
72 hours
Notification — a fuller report with an initial assessment and any mitigations users can apply.
Final
Final report — for an exploited vulnerability, no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available; for a severe incident, within one month of the 72-hour notification.

Who has to report?

  • Manufacturers of products with digital elements made available on the EU market — today.
  • Open-source stewards — organisations that provide sustained support for free and open-source software intended for commercial use — take on reporting duties from 11 December 2027.
  • Importers and distributors have their own duties to act when they learn of a vulnerability, but the Article 14 notifications are the manufacturer's responsibility.

A five-step readiness checklist

  1. Register on the SRP and set up your organisation's access before an incident — not during one.
  2. Name a decision-maker (with backups) who can authorise a report at any hour.
  3. Define "actively exploited" for your team — what evidence (threat intelligence, customer reports, CSIRT alerts) triggers the clock.
  4. Template the three reports so the 24-hour early warning is a five-minute task.
  5. Know your products. An up-to-date SBOM lets you answer "are we affected?" in minutes when a new exploited vulnerability is announced.
