CRA timeline: the key dates

The CRA does not switch on all at once. It phases in — and the obligations that arrive first are easy to miss.

Updated November 2026: the CRA reporting obligations have applied since 11 September 2026, and the ENISA Single Reporting Platform is live. The next big milestone is full application on 11 December 2027 — about a year away.

The Cyber Resilience Act was adopted in 2024 and applies in stages. Understanding the phasing matters because the reporting obligations arrived well before the full set of product requirements, and because security-by-design work needs a long runway. Here is the timeline, and where we are on it today.

10 December 2024 — Entry into force ✓

The regulation was published in the EU Official Journal and entered into force twenty days later. This started the clock and fixed the legal text manufacturers plan against.

11 June 2026 — Conformity assessment bodies ✓

The rules on notifying conformity assessment bodies (Chapter IV) applied, so that notified bodies can be designated in time to assess important (class II) and critical products before full application.

27 July 2026 — Commission implementation guidance ✓

The Commission published non-binding guidance (C(2026) 6013) on scope — including remote data processing and free and open-source software — substantial modification, support periods, reporting and risk assessment, with practical examples aimed at SMEs.

11 September 2026 — Reporting obligations apply ✓ (now in force)

Manufacturers must now report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents through the ENISA Single Reporting Platform: an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours, and a final report. This applies to products already on the market — even though full conformity is not yet mandatory. The platform was upgraded to version 1.4 on 17 November 2026.

11 December 2027 — Full application

The core of the CRA — the essential requirements, conformity assessment, CE marking, technical documentation and the Declaration of Conformity — applies. From this date, products with digital elements placed on the EU market must comply. Open-source stewards' reporting duties also start on this date.

Why December 2027 is closer than it looks

About a year sounds comfortable, but the work does not compress well:

  • Design decisions happen early. Secure update mechanisms, key storage, attack-surface reduction and logging are architectural. Adding them to a shipped product is expensive and sometimes impossible without a hardware revision.
  • Processes take time to embed. A vulnerability-handling workflow, an SBOM pipeline, a coordinated disclosure policy and documentation are organisational muscles you build over quarters, not days.
  • Product cycles are long. A device you are designing now will be on sale after December 2027 — so it needs to be CRA-ready by design today.
  • Notified bodies will be busy. If your product needs third-party assessment, expect queues as the deadline approaches.

What to do now

  • Today (reporting is live): make sure you can detect an actively exploited vulnerability, decide quickly whether it is reportable, and register on the ENISA Single Reporting Platform so a 24-hour early warning is a routine task, not a scramble.
  • Next few months: confirm scope and risk class for each product, run a gap assessment against the Annex I essential requirements, build your SBOM, and publish a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy.
  • During 2027: close design gaps, assemble technical documentation, choose your conformity route (self-assessment or notified body), and prepare the Declaration of Conformity and CE marking.
Dates change; treat the official text as authoritative. Exact application dates and any transitional arrangements are defined in Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 and related guidance. Use the milestones above to plan, and verify specifics for your situation.
